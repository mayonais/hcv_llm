Fig. 2. Knowledge-probe responses and generated housing effects. Expected directions are prespecified literature-informed benchmarks. Knowledge-probe estimates and evidence categories are LLM responses. Generated effects are paired Cohen's d_z for homeless minus stable outputs, with percentile 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. A dash indicates a non-estimable effect; CI n.e. indicates an estimable effect with an unavailable interval. Patterns and colors distinguish agreement, disagreement, non-estimability, and relationships without a fixed directional benchmark. Mixed or uncertain knowledge answers have a separate pattern.

Fig. S1. Knowledge-probe responses and generated-outcome associations. Generated associations are Spearman correlations calculated from original-prompt outputs across both housing conditions. A dash indicates an undefined correlation. Arrows and agreement classifications use unrounded values; a correlation displayed as 0.00 can have a nonzero sign. Knowledge-probe estimates and evidence categories are LLM responses. Patterns and colors use the same categories as Fig. 2.

Fig. S2. LLM and ABM correlations for ten variable pairs, displayed separately by housing condition. Circles indicate stable housing, triangles homelessness, and vertical marks the ABM reference. Each LLM and ABM correlation for a variable pair uses the same matched-agent subset. Points are descriptive correlations without confidence intervals. The main ABM MAD combines both housing conditions rather than averaging these condition-specific discrepancies.

Fig. S3. Sensitivity of ABM correlation MAD to observation timing. Panel a shows retained agent counts; panel b shows model-specific MAD values. Selected ABM snapshots are restricted to the indicated tolerance around six months after activation. Each retained agent contributes both LLM housing-condition outputs and its corresponding ABM record. Narrower windows also change the agents included. No bootstrap intervals are calculated for this sensitivity analysis.
