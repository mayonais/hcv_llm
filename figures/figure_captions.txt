Fig. 2. Knowledge-probe responses and generated housing effects. Directions are prespecified literature-informed benchmarks. Panel (a) shows LLM-reported directions, evidence strength, association estimates, and any reported intervals. Panel (b) shows original-prompt mean paired differences for homeless minus stable outputs. Counts use the indicated weekly units; proportions and binary outcomes use percentage points. The second line, agreement, is the percentage of estimable bootstrap resamples whose mean paired difference matches the fixed literature-informed direction. Each of 1,000 resamples draws profiles with replacement within the original gender, race, and injection-experience strata, retaining both housing-condition outputs; the same draws are used across LLMs (seed 42). All displayed housing differences were estimable in all 1,000 resamples. Agreement is not assessed for mixed literature expectations (agreement n/a); zero differences do not match an expected increase or decrease. A dash before a numeric zero indicates a zero mean difference; a dash alone indicates an unavailable estimate. Arrows and cell colors use the unrounded full-sample estimates. No generated housing-effect confidence intervals are displayed. Patterns and colors distinguish agreement, disagreement, non-estimability, and relationships without a fixed directional benchmark.

Fig. S1. Knowledge-probe responses and generated-outcome associations. Page 1 shows knowledge-probe responses; page 2 shows generated associations and bootstrap directional agreement. Generated associations are Spearman correlations calculated from original-prompt outputs across both housing conditions. The second line, agreement, is the percentage of estimable bootstrap resamples whose correlation matches the fixed literature-informed direction. The 1,000 stratified paired-profile resamples are the same as those used for Fig. 2. Agreement is not assessed for mixed literature expectations or relationships with no estimable resamples (agreement n/a); non-estimable resamples do not count as directional disagreement. Estimability percentages are reported separately in the bootstrap results. A dash indicates an undefined correlation. Arrows and agreement classifications use unrounded full-sample estimates; a correlation displayed as 0.00 can have a nonzero sign. Knowledge-probe directions and estimates are LLM responses. Patterns and colors use the same categories as Fig. 2, with a separate color for mixed or uncertain knowledge-probe directions.

Fig. S2. LLM-generated and CNEP+ simulated follow-up correlations for ten variable pairs, displayed separately by housing condition. Circles indicate stable housing, triangles homelessness, and vertical marks the CNEP+ reference. Each LLM and CNEP+ correlation for a variable pair uses the same matched-agent subset. Points are descriptive correlations without confidence intervals. The main CNEP+ MAD combines both housing conditions rather than averaging these condition-specific discrepancies.

Fig. S3. Sensitivity of LLM–CNEP+ correlation MAD to observation timing. Panel a shows retained agent counts; panel b shows model-specific MAD values. Selected CNEP+ snapshots are restricted to the indicated tolerance around six months after activation. Each retained agent contributes both LLM housing-condition outputs and its corresponding CNEP+ record. Narrower windows also change the agents included. No bootstrap intervals are calculated for this sensitivity analysis.
